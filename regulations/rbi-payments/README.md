## 3. Superseded or uncertain status

- **2019 TAT circular is still the live source (as far as I can tell).** The PA Master Direction (Sep 2025, para 8(a)) and the PPI Master Direction (updated 30 Sep 2026, para 16.x) both still cite DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20 "as amended from time to time". I found no consolidation or repeal. Inference: it remains operative. The RBI page for it now says "Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021 (as amended from time to time)", which is the text still on the page even though the 2021 scheme was repealed on 1 Jul 2026.
- **"Calendar days" vs "working days".** The 2019 text defines T as the calendar date. The June 2020 circular temporarily read "calendar days" as "working days" until 31 Dec 2020. That relaxation has expired.
- **2017 liability circular (DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18) is superseded** for commercial banks by paras 64-76 of the Responsible Business Conduct Directions 2025 (Nov 2025). The text of paras 64-76 repeats the 2017 table. Those paras are themselves replaced by the Third Amendment, applicable to EBTs on or after 1 Jan 2027. Until then the 3 / 4-7 / 7+ working-day table is current. Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks, RRBs and LABs are excluded from the amendment's scope (per its first paragraph).
- **Non-bank PPI issuers** are still under PPI MD para 17 (3 days zero, 4-7 days up to ₹10,000, 10-day shadow reversal, 90 days), not the 2027 bank rules.
- **Integrated Ombudsman Scheme:** RB-IOS 2026 replaced RB-IOS 2021 from 1 Jul 2026. Complaints received before that date remain under the 2021 scheme. Several RBI documents (PPI MD para 16.5, the 2019 TAT page, the 2023 CIC circular para 2(l)) still say "2021".
- **CIC compensation:** the Oct 2023 circular and the Jan 2025 Credit Information Reporting MD both carry the ₹100 per day rule. I did not confirm whether the circular was formally withdrawn; treat the MD as the consolidated source (inference).
- **Authentication Directions:** effective 1 Apr 2026. Annex 1 now lists "Tap and Pay UPI" and the 2026 e-mandate framework, so the annex was updated after Sep 2025. Cross-border CNP requirements start 1 Oct 2026.

## 4. Exact answers to the three specific questions

**Does the 2019 TAT table say P2P and P2M differently? Yes, for UPI.** The words "P2P" and "P2M" do not appear. The table distinguishes them by description:

| Row | Incident | Timeline | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4a | UPI: "Account debited but the beneficiary account is not credited (transfer of funds)." | "If unable to credit the beneficiary account, auto reversal (R) by the Beneficiary bank latest on T + 1 day." | "₹100/- per day if delay is beyond T + 1 day." |
| 4b | UPI: "Account debited but transaction confirmation not received at merchant location (payment to merchant)." | "Auto-reversal within T + 5 days." | "₹100/- per day if delay is beyond T + 5 days." |

So transfer (P2P) is T+1 and merchant payment (P2M) is T+5. Other rows: IMPS 3a T+1; ATM 1a T+5 (reversal "within a maximum of T + 5 days"); card-to-card 2a T+1; PoS and CNP 2b-2c T+5; AePS 5a-5b T+5; APBS 6a and NACH 7a T+1; PPI 8b (on-us) T+1. All compensation is ₹100 per day of delay beyond the timeline. There is no cap stated in the table.

**T+1 / ₹100 text.** Circular paras: "(a) the prescribed TAT is the outer limit for resolution of failed transactions; and (b) the banks and other operators / system participants shall endeavour towards quicker resolution" (para 4). "Wherever financial compensation is involved, the same shall be effected to the customer's account suo moto, without waiting for a complaint or claim from the customer" (para 5). Annex para 4: "T is the day of transaction and refers to the calendar date." Annex para 5: R is the day the reversal is concluded.

**Who gets the compensation (Annex general instructions, para 1).** For a credit-push transfer, the penalty is paid to the beneficiary when credit is delayed beyond the TAT. If initiation is delayed at the originator bank's end beyond the TAT, the penalty is paid to the originator.

## 5. Corrections to NIYAM's existing claims

- **Ombudsman 30-day rule.** The rule is "30 days, or the time specified by RBI, NPCI or card network guidelines, whichever is higher" (FAQ Q16 and Q17 for RB-IOS 2026). The customer may complain to the Ombudsman if there is no reply in that window, or if dissatisfied with the reply. The complaint must be filed within 90 days of the window ending or of the last communication from the entity, whichever is later. The rule is not a flat 30 days. The scheme is RB-IOS **2026** (in force 1 Jul 2026), not 2021. Compensation powers: up to ₹30 lakh for consequential loss and up to ₹3 lakh for time, expenses and harassment (FAQ Q22).
- **Bank complaint timeline (30 days) and escalation.** The Internal Ombudsman Directions 2026 (cl. 14(9)) require the bank's final decision within 30 days of receipt, with automatic internal escalation of partially resolved or rejected complaints (cl. 14(1)). The IO step must be mentioned in the reply (cl. 14(11)).
- **2019 TAT.** Confirmed as a real circular, effective 15 Oct 2019 (para 7). The brief's "₹100 per day" text is correct. NIYAM's rule cards must keep P2P (T+1) and P2M (T+5) separate. If a card merges them into one "T+1" rule, it is wrong for merchant payments.
- **Liability rule timing.** Customer-liability thresholds change for transactions from 1 Jan 2027 (3 working days becomes 5 calendar days). A card written against the 2017 rule will be wrong for 2027 test dates.
- **CIC compensation:** ₹100 per calendar day after 30 calendar days from complaint filing (2023 circular para 2(a)). The CI has 21 days of that 30 (para 2(b)). This is confirmed.
- **FREE-AI "recommendations, not binding"**: not in this folder's scope and not checked here.
